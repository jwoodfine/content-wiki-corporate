Risk disclosure is organized in this wiki around five categories, each held in a dedicated
article: market and property risk, financing and interest rate risk, development and
construction risk, structure and regulatory risk, and risk specific to the offering mechanism
itself. The organizing principle is that a risk factor belongs with the part of the business
it affects — a lender relationship, a construction schedule, a jurisdiction's legal regime, or
the mechanics of the investment unit itself — rather than in a single undifferentiated list.

## Why risks are organized this way

A single, unstructured list of risk factors makes it difficult for a reader to judge which
risks are relevant to a specific decision. An investor evaluating the [[narrow-bank-financial-model|Narrow Banking
Model]] needs to understand financing risk in detail; a counterparty assessing a specific
jurisdiction needs the structural and regulatory picture for that jurisdiction; a reader
evaluating whether to hold [[perpetual-equity-model|Perpetual Equity]] units needs to understand the offering
mechanism itself. Grouping risk factors by the part of the business they affect lets each
reader find the relevant material without wading through the full set.

The five categories are:

- [[market-and-property-risk|Market and property risk]] — vacancy, rent levels, property values, tenant concentration,
  and general economic conditions affecting commercial real estate.
- [[financing-and-interest-rate-risk|Financing and interest rate risk]] — availability of financing on favorable terms and
  exposure to interest rate movement on debt incurred after a building stabilizes.
- [[development-and-construction-risk|Development and construction risk]] — cost overruns, delays, contractor performance,
  and zoning and permitting risk during ground-up construction.
- [[structure-and-regulatory-risk|Structure and regulatory risk]] — the consequences of operating across multiple
  jurisdictions under distinct legal and tax regimes, and the risk that any of those regimes
  changes.
- [[offering-risk|Offering risk]] — risk arising from the mechanics of the investment unit itself, including
  illiquidity and concentration.

## Disclosure posture

Every article in this category states what could go wrong. None of them state or imply what
will happen, what an investor should expect to earn, or that a described structural feature
eliminates the risk it is paired with. A risk factor is a description of a contingency, not a
forecast, and its presence in this wiki is not an invitation to invest or an assessment of
whether any particular reader should invest.

Risk disclosure in this wiki does not substitute for the risk factor section of a prospectus,
an offering memorandum, or an annual information form filed with the applicable securities
regulator. Where a formal disclosure document exists for a specific offering, that document —
not this wiki — is the authoritative and complete statement of the risks applicable to that
offering. This wiki's risk articles are a plain-language orientation to the categories of risk
that generally apply to the [[direct-hold-solutions-structural-comparison|Direct-Hold Solutions]] structure; they are not
a substitute for reviewing the applicable regulatory filing before making an investment
decision.

Readers should also review [[forward-looking-statements-advisory|Forward-Looking Statements Advisory]] before relying on any
description in this category that uses language such as "planned," "intended," "may," or
"target." Those terms mark statements about the future, which are inherently uncertain and
which the company does not guarantee.
